Browse the web with your eyes and voice.

Iris is a Chrome extension for people who cannot use a mouse or keyboard. Look at a link, blink twice, and it opens. Say what you want, and Iris finds it.

Looking

People with ALS, spinal cord injuries or cerebral palsy often cannot use a mouse. Most eye-control tools move a cursor pixel by pixel, which jitters on a webcam, and click whatever the user happens to look at. Iris aims at real targets and only clicks when the user means it.

How it works

  1. 1

    Aim

    Iris numbers every link and button on the page. Your gaze snaps to the nearest one, so a webcam is accurate enough.

  2. 2

    Confirm

    A deliberate double blink opens the target. Looking alone never clicks, and anything that submits, pays or deletes needs that blink.

  3. 3

    Ask

    Say "open the history section" or "summarize this". The voice agent picks the target; you still confirm it.

  4. 4

    Rest

    Iris watches for fatigue through the camera. When it rises, Iris checks in by voice and offers a hand or a short break.

Private by default

  • Video stays home

    Camera frames and face landmarks are processed on the laptop and never leave it.

  • Body signals in memory

    Heart rate, breathing and blink data are used for slow trends and are not stored.

  • Screenshots on request

    A page screenshot goes to Gemini only when you ask, and is never kept.

  • No keys in the extension

    Every API key lives in the Worker. The extension holds none.
